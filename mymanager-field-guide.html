A-00

Welcome & Orientation

ScaleOverview
What you're looking at

My MaNaGeR is a single-page workspace for running a construction or capital project end‑to‑end — charter through closeout — without stitching together five different tools. Everything you enter feeds everything else: a task you add to the WBS shows up in the Gantt, the Kanban board, the RACI matrix, and the Dashboard's Health Score, automatically.

The app doesn't assume one delivery style. At the top of the screen you'll always find three tabs — Waterfall, Agile, and Hybrid — and the panels beneath reorganize around whichever one is active. Hover any of the three (before you pick) and a small card explains what that methodology changes and gives a worked example, so the choice isn't a guess.

The three methodologies

01 — DEFAULT

Waterfall

Sequential, date-driven delivery. WBS tasks cascade off predecessors, the Gantt shows a true critical path, and EVM (SPI/CPI) assumes a fixed baseline. Best when scope and sequence are locked before ground breaks.

02

Agile

Sprint-based delivery. Reveals the Kanban board's sprint tools and unlocks three extra meeting templates — Sprint Planning, Sprint Review, Retrospective — under the Meetings panel.

03

Hybrid

Runs both models side by side — a Waterfall schedule for procurement and structural milestones, Agile sprints for design iteration or punch-list burndown.

Methodology Lock. Once your team commits, lock it in from Settings ▸ Controls. A gold padlock appears in the header as confirmation, and the app blocks mid-project switching — the same discipline as a signed-off drawing set.

A-01

Your First 15 Minutes

ScaleProcedure
Do these in order

Everything downstream — Health Score, EVM, RACI, the Dashboard — depends on a handful of fields being filled in first. Here's the sequence that gets a new project from blank to fully live.

Turn on the Advanced Packs you need

Open Settings ▸ Advanced Packs and switch on whatever your project uses — Schedule Science for the Gantt & critical path, Money for Budget & Resources, Governance for Risk/RACI/Changes, Field for Meetings & Claim Pack. New projects start Core-only (Sheet A-00), so this is the step that reveals most of the top nav.

Pick a methodology and set the basics

Choose Waterfall, Agile, or Hybrid up top. Then open Settings ▸ Controls and set your name (personalizes the greeting) and your Work Week (3–7 days) — it feeds both Resource Utilization and Budget's labor-cost math.

Fill out the Charter

Your project's identity document — Sponsor, stakeholders, requirements, deliverables, KPIs, scope, budget. Already have one written? Use Upload Existing Charter (.docx/.pdf/.txt) or Paste Charter Text to auto-fill the fields instead of retyping.

Establish realistic target dates

Before a single task exists, open the Charter Dates Prompt in the Charter panel — it reasons through a realistic duration, a weather-season contingency, and a management reserve, then proposes dates to lock in.

Build the WBS

Paste a plain-text outline (blank line = new main task, indents = subtasks) or add tasks by hand. Set predecessors so downstream dates cascade automatically as the schedule shifts.

Set RACI

Rows and columns populate live from your WBS tasks and your Resources/Stakeholders lists — nothing to re-enter. Click a cell to cycle Responsible → Accountable → Consulted → Informed.

Save a Baseline

Settings ▸ Controls ▸ Save Baseline snapshots your current schedule and budget before real work starts — the Dashboard's Baseline Variance card has nothing to compare against until you do this once.

Open the Dashboard

Health Score, EVM, Timeline Target Tracker, and Today's Focus are now populated. Sheet A-02 breaks down every number you'll see there.

Back up to a file

LocalStorage protects you against a closed tab, not a wiped browser. Settings ▸ Controls ▸ Save Project to File exports a portable .json — do this before switching devices or closing up for the day.

A-02

Dashboard & Health Score

Scale1 : 1 detail
Where every number lives

The Dashboard is the only panel that reads from every other panel at once. Nothing here is manually entered — it's all calculated from your WBS, Budget, Risk register, and Charter the moment they have enough data to be honest about it.

One number, 0–100, blending five weighted factors. Until there's enough of the project entered to calculate honestly, it reads "no data yet" instead of showing a fake score.

FactorWeightReads from
Completion30%% complete across WBS tasks
Schedule25%Timeline Target Tracker / critical path drift
Budget20%Planned vs. actual spend
Risk15%Open, unmitigated items in the Risk register
Change volume10%Pending / recent Change Requests

This same score is what gets stamped at the top of every meeting agenda you copy from the Meetings panel — see Sheet A-09.

Earned Value Management, auto-calculated once you have at least one budget line and one dated task. SPI/CPI above 1.0 means ahead of plan / under budget; below 1.0 means behind / over.

SPI

Schedule Perf. Index

Earned value ÷ planned value.

CPI

Cost Perf. Index

Earned value ÷ actual cost.

EAC

Estimate at Completion

Forecast total cost at finish.

ETC

Estimate to Complete

What's left to spend.

Also on the card: SV (Schedule Variance), CV (Cost Variance), PV (Planned Value), AC (Actual Cost), and VAC / TCPI in the forecast strip beneath.

Timeline Target Tracker

Compares the Target Completion Date you locked into the Charter against your Current Planned Finish — the latest end date across all scheduled tasks. Once you slip past target, a badge appears in the header from any panel; clicking it jumps straight back to this card.

Baseline Variance

A different comparison: Settings ▸ Controls ▸ Save Baseline snapshots your schedule and budget at a moment in time. From then on, this card shows how far your live numbers have drifted from that snapshot — independent of the Charter's promised date.

One unified view standing in for four separate checks — a single glance instead of four cards to answer "are we going to make it."

MONTE CARLO

Hit-probability

Live odds of reaching the Charter's Target Completion Date — the same simulation engine as the full Monte Carlo panel (Sheet A-07), run as a lighter pass so the dashboard stays snappy.

WEATHER

Weather Variance

Checks weather-exposed tasks against your Weather Window (a hurricane/rainy-season range plus buffer, set in Settings) and flags any without enough cushion.

CRASH

Crash Candidates

Ranks critical-path tasks by realistic recovery potential — e.g. "add another mason crew" — while excluding anything gated by permits, curing time, or third-party lead time, since more labor can't compress those.

FLOAT

Float Watch

Lists tasks with little or no schedule slack, plus how much float has been consumed against your saved baseline. Recalculates every time you open the Dashboard.

A separate Lead-Time Tracker card sits alongside these, listing days remaining or overdue for anything tracked as a Lead-Time task (permits, procurement) — see Sheet A-04.

A same-day view of what's active right now — refreshed on demand, not on a timer, so it reflects exactly where things stood the last time you hit refresh. Sits beside the Next 3 Priority Actions list and the Active Issues & Blockers card, which is also where you'll find the one-click Weekly Digest and Report Prompt buttons covered in Sheet A-13.

A-04

WBS, Gantt & Critical Path

Scale1 : 1 detail
Where the schedule actually lives

The Work Breakdown Structure is the spine everything else attaches to. Build it once, and the Gantt, Kanban, RACI, and Dashboard all stay in sync automatically.

Three ways to build it

MANUAL

Add Task

Build row by row. Indent / outdent to nest subtasks; collapse or expand the whole tree at once.

FAST

WBS Text Import

Paste a plain outline — blank line starts a new main task, indented lines become subtasks. Paste again later and it merges by matching name instead of duplicating.

AI-ASSISTED

Import Dates for Existing WBS

Pulls your live WBS into a prompt, asks your AI for a dated version, and merges the returned dates back in — nothing duplicated. Paste the dated outline back into the modal and it matches by task name: existing tasks are updated in place, so re-running it as the schedule evolves is always safe.

Two special task types

TYPE

Lead-Time

For procurement, permits, or anything waiting on a third party. Tracked by Submitted Date / Expected Date instead of Start/End and % Complete. Shows as a dashed card in Kanban and a hatched bar in the Gantt — and feeds the Lead-Time Tracker on the Dashboard.

TYPE

Recurring

For work that runs the whole project — weekly safety talks, ongoing QC. It carries a refresh badge in the WBS and Kanban and is tagged [RECURRING] in AI prompts so it reads differently from one-off tasks. It schedules like any other row: give it dates and it cascades with the rest.

How dates cascade

Enter a Task ID in the Pred. column. When that predecessor's end date changes, every downstream task recalculates automatically — with a cycle guard, so a circular dependency (A depends on B depends on A) can't lock up the math.

conf:high / conf:low tags. When AI-generated dates come back from the "Generate Dates" prompt, each line carries a confidence badge — high for low-variance work like pouring concrete, low for anything depending on outside parties. These tags feed directly into the Monte Carlo simulator, giving high-confidence tasks a tighter risk spread and low-confidence ones a wider one.

On the Gantt itself

TOGGLE

Critical Path Highlighter

Illuminates in gold the exact chain of tasks controlling your final deadline; every non-critical task dims. Toggle it from the Gantt panel header.

PASSIVE

Deadline Pulse Alert

Any task within 3 days of its end date pulses amber — on the Gantt bar and its Kanban card. No setup required.

The Audit → Correct → Reimport loop. Run the Schedule Audit prompt (Sheet A-13), feed its findings back into the Generate Dates prompt as context, reimport the corrected outline. This is a manual refinement cycle you drive as the schedule evolves — the app won't run it for you.

Every task, one source of truth

The WBS you build once shows up on the Gantt, the Kanban board, and the RACI matrix — never re-entered.

A-07

Risk, Issues & Simulation

Scale1 : 1 detail
The register that feeds the Health Score

Fifteen percent of your Health Score comes straight from what's open here — so this panel earns its keep beyond just documentation.

WORKFLOW

Risk → Issue conversion

Toggle the switch in the → Issue column and the row visually converts, moving to the Live Issues table with a red alert border. No re-entering the description.

SIMULATION

Monte Carlo Simulator

Runs 1,000 randomized trials of your schedule using each task's duration, your Risk Factor setting, and predecessor chains, then reports P10/P50/P80/P90 completion dates — a realistic range instead of one falsely-precise date. Set a Target Completion Date and it reports your probability of hitting it. Needs at least 2 dated tasks.

Where the spread comes from. Tasks tagged conf:high during AI-assisted dating (Sheet A-04) get a tighter risk spread in the simulation; conf:low tasks get a wider one — instead of every task sharing one blanket Risk Factor.

A-10

Voice Capture & Field Reports

Scale1 : 1 detail
Talk the minutes, not type them

Voice capture turns a running meeting into a written record on the spot — no phone recorder, no second screen. The transcript lands on the meeting's own record in project state, so it travels with the project file and can feed everything downstream (Sheet A-11 shows one very useful destination).

Two capture tiers — one button

TIER 0

Live captions (Web Speech API)

Streams captions to the screen in real time while you talk. Needs a network connection and a browser with speech recognition — but it's circuit-broken: if recognition is unavailable or errors mid-meeting, capture keeps going and the transcript stays editable, so a dropped connection never ends your meeting or loses what you said.

TIER 1

Offline whisper (batch-on-stop)

A small offline speech model (whisper, no API key) transcribes the session when you stop capture — batch, not live. Live captions stream while you record; the full transcript is finalized on stop and saved to the meeting record. Works with zero keys and no account.

After capture

The transcript is stored on the meeting record in unified state — it survives refresh, exports with the project file, and syncs like any other field. The app runs a rule-based (zero-AI) extraction over the text to pull out decisions and action items you can confirm before the meeting closes out (Sheet A-09 covers Start/End and the Comms auto-log).

Review before you trust. Transcripts are a working record, not a court reporter. In noisy or overlapping audio — a busy site, people talking over each other — detail can be lost or misheard. Skim the transcript against your memory of the meeting before it becomes the record of record.

A-11

Claim Pack & Voice-to-Claim

Scale1 : 1 detail
The delay story, compiled while it happens

A Claim Pack is a counsel-ready package built entirely from your project's own records — weather delays, schedule slips, liquidated-damages exposure, change control, and meeting decisions for a date range you choose. Nothing is re-typed; every section is pulled live from state.

Generate a package

WINDOW

Pick From / To dates

In the Claim Pack tab, set the window you're claiming. The pack then scopes everything — slips, weather delays, LD exposure, changes, decisions — to that range.

GENERATE

Generate Package

One click compiles the full package: weather delay days, affected WBS tasks, LD/contract exposure (avoided vs incurred), baseline-vs-actual deltas, change control, and meeting decisions/action items. Copy All gets it into Word or your counsel's inbox.

Schedule Slip Log & cause tagging

Every task that slipped past its baseline end date appears in the Schedule Slip Log. Causes auto-tag from evidence — a weather delay on the same dates, a predecessor slip, a change request — and default to unknown where there's no evidence, so the log never silently pretends it knows. You can re-tag any slip manually with the dropdown. The Liquidated Damages Rollup splits exposure into avoided (weather cause) vs incurred (everything else) straight from those tags.

Voice-to-Claim

This is where a captured meeting (Sheet A-10) earns its keep: a messy on-site dispute or a weather-stop conversation becomes a transcript, the rule-based extractor pulls the decisions and action items, and those land in state where the Claim Pack can cite them. Capture in the meeting, claim from the record — no re-narration.

Built from your records, on purpose. The Claim Pack never asks the AI to invent a delay — it compiles what you already logged. The 13th AI preset, Claim Pack (Sheet A-13), grounds an LLM in the same package for a written argument if you want one.

A-13

AI Assistant

Scale1 : 1 detail
Presets13
One master switch. Three engine tiers.

The AI Assistant compiles your live project data into structured output — reports, dates, audits, claim arguments. One master switch in the Settings drawer turns the whole assistant on or off, and the engine tier (Off / Local / Cloud) sits in the AI window header so you always know which one is live.

The three tiers

OFF

Off

The assistant is disabled and the floating AI button is hidden. Everything else in the app is untouched.

LOCAL

Local — zero-key

Runs in your browser, offline, with no API key and no account. "Zero-fabrication by construction": every line it writes traces to a real field in your project state — it derives, it doesn't invent numbers.

CLOUD

Cloud — your own key

Optional BYO-key tier that calls out to your chosen provider — OpenAI, Google Gemini, or Anthropic Claude — from the AI window's connection settings; Connect & Test verifies the key live before Send unlocks. Your live project data is compiled into the prompt that leaves the browser — nothing else does. If a provider rate-limits the current model (HTTP 429/503), the app automatically retries on the next smaller model in that provider's fallback ladder — e.g. Gemini 2.0 Flash → Flash Lite → Flash Latest — and shows an amber “Fell back to …” badge naming the model that answered. A rejected key (401/403) is cleared immediately; it never quietly falls through to a smaller model with a bad key.

The Settings-drawer switch and the header tier selector are the same control: switch the assistant Off and the tier is off; switch it On and it restores Local or your last tier. One control, one meaning — the floating AI button can never be visible while the engine is off.

Copy-first and agent-style — both still work

Every preset offers both flows. The chip loads the compiled prompt into the chat box — send it on the Cloud tier, or copy prompt + context to use any AI tool you already have; that copy path never goes away. The run button is agent-style: it generates the result and writes it back into project state under the preset's name, where it travels with the project file. Ten presets have a built-in Local generator that needs no key; the other three (Visual Output, Charter Dates, Generate Dates) need the Cloud tier for the run button — on Local they'll say so and stay copy-first. Generate Dates' returned outline is merged into your WBS through the Import Dates modal in the WBS panel (Sheet A-04), so nothing overwrites your schedule without you reviewing it.

The Daily Field Report's diff. A-15

Data, Backup & Access

Scale1 : 1 detail
Your project lives on your device — and goes where you go

Every change autosaves to this browser's LocalStorage the moment you make it — no save button, no network call. By default one project lives on one browser, on one device. Three deliberate paths move it anywhere else: the portable .json file, a USB/sync transfer, or — if you want it — the optional cloud backup. The app never requires an account; everything below is a choice.

EXPORT

Save Project to File

Settings ▸ Controls ▸ Save Project to File writes a portable .json — your ticket to a new device, a teammate's laptop, or just peace of mind. Works everywhere, account or not.

IMPORT

Load Project from File

Settings ▸ Controls ▸ Load Project from File, then pick a previously saved .json to restore it.

SNAPSHOT

Save Baseline

A point-in-time snapshot of schedule and budget, feeding the Dashboard's Baseline Variance card (Sheet A-02).

Real-world moves: three scenarios

USB

Moving devices

Export to .json, carry it on a stick or email it to yourself, open the new browser, Load Project from File. Done — no account needed.

MOBILE

Viewing on the phone

The same .json opens in any browser. Since every field carries a per-field edit timestamp, when you take a copy back to the office, edits merge — newest write wins per field, not whole-file last-save.

LOSS

Device lost or wiped

If a copy was exported, saved to the cloud, or synced, the project survives the device. This is the single most important habit: a closed tab is fine, a wiped browser is not — back it up before anything drastic.

Optional cloud backup — an owner code, an editor code, or a view-only code

The Cloud Backup section in Settings ▸ Controls (right beside Drive) links a project to the backend (D1 + R2) so its state JSON lives off-device and can be pulled back on any device. It is strictly opt-in per project, never required, and never gates a feature — the JSON export/import path above remains the guaranteed route on every device, signed in or not.

OWNER

Owner code (create)

Create Cloud Project generates a 16-character owner/recovery code shown EXACTLY ONCE — copy it and store it somewhere safe. You authenticate saves/loads with it; the plaintext is never stored server-side (only a salted hash), so a lost code is unrecoverable without the linked Google account.

EDITOR

Editor codes (scoped)

The owner can generate editor codes with a label and tick which sections each code may WRITE (WBS, Budget, Risks, Meetings…). Scope is enforced SERVER-SIDE on every save — an editor (or a leaked code) physically cannot touch anything outside its grant, and out-of-scope panels grey out as a visual hint. Revoke a code anytime; it stops working immediately.

VIEWER

View-only access codes

Access codes on app.html unlock projects read-only (view-only code = read-only project), independent of cloud — the app gate that protects every project. Cloud editor codes are about who may WRITE which sections; view codes are about who may OPEN the project at all.

Save, load, recover, and unlink

Save to Cloud / Load from Cloud

With the owner (or an editor) code in session, Save to Cloud pushes the current state; Load from Cloud replaces this device's workspace with the stored snapshot (it asks first — current local data would be overwritten). Each save records a changelog entry with field-level before/after values (or a snapshot for bulk changes).

On another device? Enter the code

Load with Code accepts a 16-character owner or editor code, pulls the snapshot, and keeps the credential for this session only. A fresh device just needs the code typed in — no account required for owner or editor access.

Recover Owner Code (lost code)

Recovery is gated on the Google account that was linked when the project was created: sign in with Google (optional identity, never required) and Recover Owner Code issues a NEW owner code — the old one stops working immediately, and the recovery itself is logged. Without the linked account, a lost owner code cannot be recovered; editor codes can always be re-issued by the owner.

Changelog — view and revert

The owner can open the changelog to see who changed what and when (Owner or "Editor — label", field-level diffs, or "Full-state change" for bulk operations). Revert is owner-only and never erases history: it writes the recorded before-values back (or restores the snapshot) and logs the revert itself as a new entry — so a revert of a revert restores the pre-revert state.

Unlink from Cloud

Unlink from Cloud deletes the CLOUD copy (snapshot, editor codes, changelog) and stops syncing — this device keeps its local data, so nothing you have here is lost. It is owner-only and irreversible for the cloud side; re-create the cloud project later if you change your mind.

Optional Google identity — gates recovery, not the app

Signing in with Google is entirely optional. It unlocks two conveniences: Google Drive backup/restore of your project files, and owner-code recovery for cloud projects. It never gates anything — create, save, load, and editor access all work with zero account. You can sign in later, or never.

Copy All — your fastest way into Word

Navigate to any panel and click Copy All. Everything visible on that view is compiled, timestamped, and placed on your clipboard — ready to paste straight into a Word document, an email, or a client report. It works the same way on every panel: WBS, Budget, Risk, RACI, Charter, all of it.

Safety net: Clear All

Hold the Clear All button for a full 10 seconds — a red bar fills across the bottom as you hold — and a 5-second undo window appears immediately after, so an accidental clear is always recoverable.

A-16

Weather & Forecast

ScaleOverview
Rain before it costs you a day

The Weather Risk Forecast panel pulls a live forecast for your site (Open-Meteo, cached 3 hours) and flags the days that matter to outdoor work — rain ≥60%, heat ≥32°C, cold ≤0°C — against the tasks that are weather-sensitive.

SITE

Set your site once

Type the site city and hit Locate to geocode it. No location set? The forecast is a regional estimate, not site-specific — honest about that until you set one.

WINDOW

7-day / 16-day view

Toggle the horizon the forecast strip covers. The flags work the same either way: a day is a risk day when it clears a threshold and sits inside the next 7 days or overlaps a weather-sensitive task — and the Dashboard's buffer checks read the same flags.

LOG

Weather Delay Log

Log a delay day — date, condition, affected tasks — and it flows straight into the Schedule Slip Log and LD exposure math (Sheet A-11). Weather-caused slips read as avoided exposure.

The Dashboard's Weather Variance card (Sheet A-02) cross-checks weather-exposed tasks against your Weather Window — the hurricane/rainy-season range plus buffer you set in Settings — and flags anything without enough cushion. The whole panel is a toggle: Settings ▸ Feature flags ▸ Weather Forecast Panel hides or reveals it.

Forecast, not fortune-telling. Until a site location is set, weather windows are regional estimates. Set your site, review the flagged days weekly, and treat the forecast as an early-warning tool — the delay log is what actually counts on the claim side.

A-18

When Something Looks Off

ScaleTroubleshooting
Before you assume it's broken

Three plain-language checks cover most "it's not working" moments. Do them in order.

Hard refresh after an update

The app serves cached files first, so after a version update a normal refresh can keep showing the old screen — a setting appears to do nothing, or a panel you know exists is missing. A hard refresh (Ctrl+Shift+R / Cmd+Shift+R) pulls the new version. This is the fix for most "my change didn't apply" moments.

Find the error log

The app keeps a client-side error log you can open from the UI and copy to your clipboard. If something misbehaves, Copy log and paste it into your bug report — it saves the person helping you a full round of questions.

Check whether you're view-only

A project opened with a view-only access code blocks edits — toggles and save actions are rejected with a notice instead of silently working. If a switch flips and immediately flips back, you're almost certainly in view-only mode with the wrong code.
